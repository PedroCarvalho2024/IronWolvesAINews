## New techniques / papers

- **Why tool-using agents fail: they act before they have the evidence**
  Across ten model setups and a new 656-case benchmark, single actions were usually fine, but multi-step workflows broke because agents stopped investigating too early or acted on prerequisites they had not checked.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07753

- **ReFold shrinks long agent conversations without losing history**
  A training-free layer that folds old turns into a compact, reversible form, cutting tokens by up to 2.5x and KV-cache memory (the model's per-session working memory) in half with no drop in task success.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07863

## RAG / vector DB tooling

- **Content extraction should be its own stage in your RAG (feeding docs to an LLM) pipeline**
  On 180 mixed enterprise documents, the best extractor scored 97.4/100 and a structure-aware chunker found the right chunk in the top 10 results 92.8% of the time, a reminder that the retriever cannot fix bad parsing.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07091

## AI security & compliance

- **APEX blocks prompt injection by checking what the task allows**
  Instead of spotting attacks, it only lets an agent call tools, MCP servers or skills when the action is justified by a contract compiled from the task, reaching 0% attack success on five of six benchmarks, and the code is public.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06966

- **Malicious agent skills must first win the retrieval race, and attackers can tune for it**
  Known skill injections lost 87 to 97% of their power when they had to compete with other skills to get picked by the router, but a new method called CORSA optimises them to get retrieved first, so do not treat skill routing as a defence.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08098
